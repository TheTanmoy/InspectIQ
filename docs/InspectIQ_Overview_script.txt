1. Meet InspectIQ. Quality reports that write themselves, while people still make every decision.

2. In a typical plant, an inspector spends about two hours on every quality report. That is 234 reports a month, built by hand from sensors, cameras, checklists and supplier certificates. The result is often vague, late, and unsigned.

3. InspectIQ splits the work. Code computes every number. The AI writes the words. Guardrails check what the AI wrote. And people sign it off.

4. Inspection data streams in live from three production lines, and every lot is traced back to the raw material heat it was made from.

5. One click generates a standard report in seconds, with its purpose, scope, defects and measurements already filled in.

6. Here, the AI links rising pitting on the piston rod line to one steel heat whose sulphur was outside its certificate. A material problem, not a people problem.

7. The report then moves through the sign-off chain. Inspector, supervisor, quality manager, and for high risk lots, the plant quality head. Nobody can sign twice, and blocking compliance rules stop approval.

8. Across the plant, the dashboard spots patterns automatically: a porosity spike after a mould change, a drifting tool, an expired gauge.

9. IT can see every token it costs. The whole system runs for about 43 thousand rupees a month, and on our assumptions it pays back in 4 months.

10. InspectIQ. Hours of paperwork, now minutes of review.
